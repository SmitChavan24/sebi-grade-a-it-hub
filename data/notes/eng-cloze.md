# Cloze Test, Fillers and Sentence Completion

## Cloze test

A passage with blanks; the correct word must fit **grammar, meaning and tone** simultaneously.

**Method:**
1. **Read the whole passage first** for the theme and tone before filling anything. Later blanks often clarify earlier ones.
2. Fill the **easy blanks first** — they narrow the context for the hard ones.
3. For each blank ask: what **part of speech** is needed? Then, does the sentence need a **contrast, continuation, cause or example**?
4. Re-read the completed passage at the end. A wrong word usually *sounds* wrong in the flow.

## Connector logic — the highest-return skill here

| Relationship | Signals |
|---|---|
| **Contrast** | but, however, although, though, yet, nevertheless, whereas, despite, in spite of, on the contrary, conversely |
| **Continuation / addition** | and, moreover, furthermore, in addition, besides, likewise, similarly |
| **Cause** | because, since, as, due to, owing to, on account of |
| **Effect** | therefore, hence, thus, consequently, as a result, accordingly |
| **Example** | for instance, for example, such as, namely |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that, in case |
| **Concession** | granted that, even if, admittedly |
| **Sequence** | first, subsequently, finally, meanwhile |

Most cloze and filler questions reduce to identifying which of these relationships the sentence needs. Get the relationship right and usually only one option survives.

## Double fillers

Both blanks must work. Strategy: **find the blank you are more certain about**, eliminate on that one alone, then check the survivors against the second blank. Do not try to solve both at once.

## Sentence completion / improvement

Common error patterns to check in order:
1. **Subject-verb agreement**
2. **Tense consistency**
3. **Preposition** (the most common single error in Indian competitive English)
4. **Parallelism** in lists and comparisons
5. **Misplaced modifier**
6. **Redundancy** — "return back", "repeat again", "advance planning", "free gift", "cancel out"
7. **Article usage** — a/an/the

## Word-pair sense

Many options are near-synonyms with different registers or connotations. Distinguish:
- *affect* (verb, to influence) vs *effect* (noun, result; verb, to bring about)
- *comprise* (the whole comprises the parts — never "comprised of") vs *compose*
- *imply* (speaker) vs *infer* (listener)
- *principal* (main, or head) vs *principle* (rule)
- *ensure* (make certain) vs *insure* (insurance) vs *assure* (tell someone confidently)
- *eminent* (distinguished) vs *imminent* (about to happen)
- *stationary* (not moving) vs *stationery* (paper)
- *complement* (completes) vs *compliment* (praise)

---

## Exam pointers

1. **Read the whole cloze passage before filling any blank.**
2. **Connector logic solves most of these questions** — learn the table.
3. In double fillers, eliminate on the blank you are **surer** about.
4. "Comprised of" is **always wrong**.
5. Redundancy is the trap examiners most enjoy: "return back", "reason is because", "more better".
