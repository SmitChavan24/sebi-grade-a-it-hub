# Para Jumbles and Sentence Rearrangement

## The method

1. **Find the opening sentence.** It introduces the subject by **full name or noun**, contains no pronoun without an antecedent, and no connector like *however, therefore, moreover, this, these, such, also*.
2. **Find mandatory pairs.** Two sentences that must be adjacent because one supplies a noun the other refers to by pronoun, or because a connector demands an immediate predecessor.
3. **Use transition words** to order the middle: *first… then… finally*; *however* must follow a contrasting statement; *for example* must follow a general claim; *therefore* must follow a reason.
4. **Find the closing sentence.** It concludes, summarises, or states a consequence — often containing *thus, hence, finally, in conclusion*, or a general statement after specifics.
5. **Check the options.** Often you need only one confident pair to eliminate three of four options. Do not construct the whole sequence if elimination is faster.

## Chronology and logic markers

- **Time:** dates, years, *initially, later, subsequently, eventually, then*.
- **Cause before effect** — always.
- **General before specific** — the claim, then the example.
- **Problem → cause → solution → result** is the commonest structure in these passages.
- **Article shift:** the **first** mention of a noun usually takes "a/an"; later mentions take "the" or a pronoun. *A committee was formed… **The committee** recommended…* This alone often fixes the order of two sentences.

## Pronoun chains

Pronouns (*it, they, this, these, such, he, its*) **cannot appear before their antecedent**. Scanning for the sentence that introduces the noun, and then for the sentence using the pronoun, produces a forced pair more often than any other technique.

## Common traps

- Two sentences that both look like openers — check which one the other depends on.
- A connector at the start of a sentence that seems to fit two places — check which preceding sentence it genuinely contrasts with.
- Options designed so that the first sentence is correct in three of them; in that case the discrimination is at the end, so **work backwards from the closing sentence**.

## Practice discipline

Do 10 sets a day for two weeks and the pattern recognition becomes automatic. Time yourself: **60–75 seconds per set** is the target; if you exceed two minutes, mark and move on.

---

## Exam pointers

1. **Openers have no pronouns and no connectors.**
2. **A pronoun can never precede its antecedent** — this creates the mandatory pairs.
3. The **a/an → the** article shift orders first and later mentions.
4. **Eliminate using options**; do not always build the full sequence.
5. If the openers are identical across options, **solve from the end**.
