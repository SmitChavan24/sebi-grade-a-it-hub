# Current Affairs — The Method, Not the Material

> Most candidates fail current affairs not from lack of reading but from lack of a system. This note is the system. Follow it for 180 days and you will out-perform people who read three times as much.

## 1. The window

SEBI questions cluster in the **6 months before the exam**, with a heavier weight on the **last 3 months**. Anything older matters only if it is still live policy. If the notification is out, that fixes your window immediately.

## 2. What actually gets asked

In descending order of return:

1. **SEBI itself** — new regulations and amendments, consultation papers, board meeting outcomes, big enforcement orders, new frameworks, committee reports, leadership.
2. **Securities market events** — index milestones, new instruments, settlement cycle changes, MII developments, large IPOs, FPI/DII flows.
3. **RBI and banking** — policy rate decisions, new frameworks, payment system developments.
4. **Economy** — budget numbers, GDP/inflation prints, major schemes.
5. **Technology in finance** — CERT-In directions, DPDP rules, AI/blockchain in the market, cyber incidents at regulated entities. *For an IT-stream candidate this rises to number 2.*
6. Generic national/international affairs, awards, sports, appointments — lowest return per hour.

## 3. The daily 20-minute routine

```
5 min  SEBI website: Press Releases + latest Circulars (headlines only)
7 min  One business paper: markets + economy pages
5 min  Log 3-5 items in the Current Affairs page of this app
3 min  Skim yesterday's log
```

Weekly, on your revision day: **compile the week into one page** and delete anything that did not survive the week.

## 4. The note format that works

For each item, write exactly four lines:

```
WHAT:   One sentence on what happened.
WHO:    Which regulator/body/company, and under which regulation or Act.
WHY:    The problem it solves.
SO WHAT: The one line you would say in an interview.
```

Four lines force you to process rather than copy. An item you cannot compress to four lines is one you did not understand.

## 5. Sources, ranked

**Primary (read these):** sebi.gov.in press releases, circulars, orders, board meeting outcomes, annual report; rbi.org.in press releases and the Financial Stability Report; PIB; CERT-In advisories; MeitY.

**Secondary (skim these):** Mint, Business Standard, Economic Times markets pages.

**Tertiary (revision only):** monthly current-affairs PDFs from any coaching source. Never read these first — they strip out the reasoning you need for the interview.

## 6. The interview multiplier

Phase III is largely a current-affairs conversation with your CV attached. A candidate who can say *"SEBI's CSCRF, issued in August 2024, consolidated the earlier cyber circulars and moved regulated entities to a graded, outcome-based model"* is in a different category from one who says *"SEBI has cyber rules"*. Your daily log is what produces the first sentence.

Keep a separate short list of **10 SEBI developments you can discuss for two minutes each**. Rehearse them aloud. That list, plus your own projects, is 60% of the interview.

---

## Exam pointers

1. Six months back, with the last three months weighted heaviest.
2. SEBI's own website beats every compilation — it is the source those compilations copy.
3. Four lines per item: WHAT / WHO / WHY / SO WHAT.
4. Compile weekly; revise monthly; never leave compilation to the last fortnight.
5. For the IT stream, treat CERT-In, DPDP and SEBI's technology circulars as core current affairs, not as optional extras.
