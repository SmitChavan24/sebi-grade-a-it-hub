# IT Act 2000, CERT-In Directions and the DPDP Act

## Information Technology Act, 2000

India's primary cyber law, amended substantially in **2008**. It gives legal recognition to **electronic records and digital/electronic signatures**, and creates offences and penalties.

**Key sections:**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **43** | Penalty and compensation for damage to a computer/system — unauthorised access, downloading, virus introduction, denial of access, **civil liability** |
| **43A** | **Body corporate handling sensitive personal data must maintain "reasonable security practices"**; compensation for negligence (largely superseded in effect by the DPDP Act — verify the current position) |
| **65** | Tampering with computer source documents — 3 years and/or ₹2 lakh |
| **66** | Computer-related offences (dishonestly or fraudulently doing anything in S.43) — **3 years and/or ₹5 lakh** |
| **66B** | Dishonestly receiving a stolen computer resource |
| **66C** | **Identity theft** — 3 years and ₹1 lakh |
| **66D** | **Cheating by personation using a computer resource** (the section used for most online fraud) |
| **66E** | Violation of privacy — capturing/publishing private images |
| **66F** | **Cyber terrorism** — imprisonment up to **life** |
| **67 / 67A / 67B** | Publishing obscene / sexually explicit material / child sexual abuse material electronically |
| **69** | Power to **intercept, monitor or decrypt** in the interest of sovereignty, security, public order etc. |
| **69A** | Power to **block** public access to information |
| **69B** | Monitoring and collecting traffic data for cyber security |
| **70** | **Protected system** — declared critical information infrastructure; **NCIIPC** is the nodal agency |
| **70B** | **CERT-In** as the national nodal agency for incident response |
| **72 / 72A** | Breach of confidentiality and privacy; disclosure of information in breach of a lawful contract |
| **79** | **Safe harbour** for intermediaries, conditional on due diligence |
| **43, 46** | **Adjudicating Officer**; appeals to **TDSAT** (which absorbed the Cyber Appellate Tribunal) |

**Institutions:** **CCA** (Controller of Certifying Authorities, for digital signatures), **CERT-In** (S.70B), **NCIIPC** (critical infrastructure, S.70), **I4C** and the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** (cybercrime.gov.in).

## CERT-In Directions (April 2022, under S.70B(6))

Mandatory for service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations:

1. **Report specified cyber incidents within 6 hours** of noticing or being notified.
2. **Synchronise system clocks** to NPL/NIC NTP servers.
3. **Maintain ICT system logs for 180 days**, within Indian jurisdiction.
4. **Data centres, VPS, cloud and VPN providers** must retain subscriber/customer registration data for **five years** after cancellation.
5. **Virtual asset service providers** must maintain KYC and transaction records for five years.
6. Designate a **point of contact** for CERT-In.

> The **six-hour reporting window** is the single most-asked fact from this direction. Know it, and be able to contrast it with the DPDP Act's breach-notification obligation and SEBI's own incident reporting timelines for regulated entities.

## Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP)

India's data protection law, enacted **11 August 2023**, applying to digital personal data.

**Key concepts:**
- **Data Principal** — the individual. **Data Fiduciary** — who determines the purpose and means. **Data Processor** — processes on behalf of a fiduciary. **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)** — notified on the basis of volume and sensitivity, with extra obligations: a **Data Protection Officer based in India**, an independent **data auditor**, and **Data Protection Impact Assessments**.
- **Lawful bases:** **consent** (free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, with clear affirmative action) and **certain legitimate uses**.
- **Notice** must accompany consent; consent must be **withdrawable** as easily as it was given. **Consent Managers** are registered with the Board.
- **Rights of the Data Principal:** access, correction and erasure, grievance redressal, nomination. **Duties** are also imposed on principals (a notable feature of this Act).
- **Children:** verifiable parental consent for under-18s; no tracking or targeted advertising at children.
- **Obligations:** purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, **reasonable security safeguards**, **breach notification to the Board and affected principals**, erasure when the purpose is served.
- **Cross-border transfer:** permitted except to countries **restricted** by the Central Government (a negative-list approach).
- **Data Protection Board of India** — adjudicates; appeals lie to **TDSAT**.
- **Penalties:** up to **₹250 crore** for failure to take reasonable security safeguards; up to ₹200 crore for breach-notification failures — no compensation to individuals is provided under the Act itself.

> Implementation is being phased through rules; **verify the current notified status** before quoting timelines in an interview.

## How this connects to SEBI

A SEBI-regulated entity is simultaneously: a **body corporate under the IT Act**, a **Data Fiduciary under DPDP**, subject to **CERT-In directions**, and subject to **SEBI's own cyber framework**. An answer that notes this **overlapping compliance burden** — and the need to reconcile different reporting timelines — is exactly the kind of answer an IT-stream panel is looking for.

## Exam pointers

1. **CERT-In: 6 hours to report, 180 days of logs, 5 years of subscriber data.**
2. **S.66F cyber terrorism can carry life imprisonment**; S.70 = protected system (NCIIPC).
3. DPDP: **₹250 crore** maximum penalty; appeals to **TDSAT**.
4. **CERT-In is under S.70B**; CCA handles digital signatures.
5. Note the **multi-regulator overlap** — it is the sophisticated point in any answer here.
