# Grammar — The Rules That Actually Get Tested

## 1. Subject-verb agreement

- **The verb agrees with the subject, not with the nearest noun.** *The list of items **is** on the desk.*
- Phrases like *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, besides, accompanied by* do **not** change the number of the subject. *The manager, along with his staff, **is** attending.*
- **Either/or, neither/nor** — the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. *Neither the director nor the officers **were** informed.*
- **Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, anyone** are **singular**. *Each of the candidates **has** submitted.*
- **Collective nouns** take a singular verb when acting as a unit, plural when the members act individually.
- **"A number of" is plural; "the number of" is singular.**
- Uncountable nouns (information, furniture, advice, equipment, luggage, machinery, news, scenery) are **singular** and have no plural form. *News **is** good.*
- Nouns plural in form but singular in meaning: mathematics, physics, economics, politics, statistics (as a subject).
- Sums of money, distances and periods of time take a **singular** verb. *Ten lakh rupees **is** a large amount.*

## 2. Tenses

| Use | Tense |
|---|---|
| Habitual action, universal truth | Simple present |
| Action in progress now | Present continuous |
| Past action with present relevance; "since"/"for" | **Present perfect** |
| Action completed before another past action | **Past perfect** |
| Definite past time (yesterday, in 2019, ago) | **Simple past — never present perfect** |

- *Since* + a point in time; *for* + a duration. **Since 2020 / for three years.**
- In a conditional: *If + past perfect, ... would have + past participle* (third conditional). *If he **had studied**, he **would have passed**.*
- After *"It is time"*, *"I wish"*, *"as if"* → use the **past subjunctive**. *I wish I **were** there.*

## 4. Parallelism

Items joined by *and, or, both…and, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also* must be the **same grammatical form**.
✗ *He likes reading, to write and jogging.* ✓ *He likes reading, writing and jogging.*
With **not only…but also**, whatever follows *not only* must be matched in form after *but also*.

## 5. Modifiers

A modifier must sit next to what it modifies.
✗ *Walking to the station, the rain started.* (the rain was not walking)
✓ *Walking to the station, I was caught in the rain.*
This is a **dangling modifier**, and it is a favourite in error-spotting sets.

## 6. Pronouns

Clear antecedent, correct case (*between you and **me***, not *I*), agreement in number (*Every student must submit **his or her** form*, or recast as plural), and *who* (subject) vs *whom* (object).

## 7. Articles

*A* before consonant **sounds** (*a university, a one-rupee coin*); *an* before vowel **sounds** (*an hour, an MBA, an honest man*). **The** for specific or unique things, superlatives, ordinals, rivers, ranges, oceans, and plural country names.

## 8. Common redundancies

return back · repeat again · revert back · advance planning · free gift · new innovation · final conclusion · basic fundamentals · cancel out · join together · sufficient enough · reason is because · more superior.

## Exam pointers

1. **The verb agrees with the subject, not the nearest noun.**
2. *Either/or, neither/nor* → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
3. **Superior/inferior/prefer take "to", never "than".**
4. **Definite past time cannot take the present perfect.**
5. Check **parallelism and dangling modifiers** in every error-spotting question — they are planted constantly.
