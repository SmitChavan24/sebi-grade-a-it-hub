# Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA)

> The oldest of the three securities statutes. It answers two questions: *what is a security*, and *who may run a stock exchange*.

## 1. Purpose

Enacted to **prevent undesirable transactions in securities** by regulating the business of dealing in them. Before SEBI existed it was administered by the Central Government; today SEBI administers most of it by delegation.

## 2. Section 2(h) — definition of "securities"

Securities **include**:

1. **shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures, debenture stock** or other marketable securities of a like nature in or of any incorporated company or **other body corporate**;
   - (ia) **derivatives**;
   - (ib) **units or any other instrument issued by any collective investment scheme**;
   - (ic) **security receipts** (as defined in the SARFAESI Act);
   - (id) units issued to investors under any **mutual fund** scheme;
   - (ie) units or instruments issued by an **InvIT / REIT**;
2. **Government securities**;
3. such other instruments as the **Central Government** declares to be securities;
4. **rights or interest in securities**.

**Not securities:** bank fixed deposits, insurance policies, and physical commodities — though **commodity derivatives** became securities when the **Forward Markets Commission merged into SEBI on 28 September 2015**.

## 3. Other key definitions

- **2(i) spot delivery contract** — delivery and payment on the same day or the next day. Spot delivery sits **outside** most SCRA prohibitions, which is why off-market transfers work.
- **2(ac) derivative** — a security derived from a debt instrument, share, loan, risk instrument or contract for differences; includes commodity derivatives.
- **2(j) stock exchange** — a body of individuals or a **corporatised and demutualised** entity assisting in buying, selling and dealing in securities.

## 4. Recognition and governance of exchanges

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **3** | Application for recognition |
| **4** | **Grant of recognition** |
| **4A / 4B** | **Corporatisation and demutualisation** |
| **5** | Withdrawal of recognition |
| **9** | Power of exchanges to make **bye-laws** |
| **11** | Power to **supersede** an exchange's governing body |
| **12** | Power to **suspend business** of an exchange |
| **13** | Contracts in **notified areas** — only between members |
| **16** | Power to prohibit contracts in certain cases |
| **18** | Exemptions — spot delivery contracts |
| **21** | **Conditions for listing** |
| **21A** | **Delisting** by a stock exchange |
| **23** | **Offences and penalties** |
| **23A–23H** | Monetary penalties for specific defaults |
| **23L** | **Appeal to SAT** |
| **30** | Rule-making power → **SCRR, 1957** |

**Demutualisation** (S.4A/4B) separates **ownership, management and trading rights**. Before it, brokers owned and ran the exchange they traded on — a structural conflict of interest. Post-demutualisation, at least **51% of an exchange's equity must be held by the public** (non-trading members) and exchanges became for-profit corporate entities.

## 5. SCRR, 1957 — the operational rules

**Rule 19(2)(b) and Rule 19A — Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS)**

- Every listed company must maintain **at least 25% public shareholding**.
- Certain categories (including some PSUs) have had extended timelines; the minimum offer at IPO varies by post-issue market capitalisation, with a timeline to reach 25%.
- Verify the current slabs before the exam — they have changed more than once.

## 6. How the three Acts fit together

| Act | Answers |
|---|---|
| **SCRA 1956** | What is a security? Who may run an exchange? What are listing obligations? |
| **SEBI Act 1992** | Who regulates? What powers, penalties and appeals exist? |
| **Depositories Act 1996** | How are securities held and transferred electronically? |

The **Companies Act, 2013** sits alongside for issue of shares, prospectus and corporate governance — which is why LODR and the Companies Act sometimes overlap.

## Exam pointers

1. Derivatives, CIS units, MF units, security receipts and REIT/InvIT units **are** securities under 2(h).
2. **Spot delivery** = same day or next day.
3. **4A/4B = demutualisation · 21 = listing · 23L = appeal to SAT**.
4. **MPS = 25%** under SCRR Rule 19A.
5. FMC merged into SEBI on **28 September 2015**; the Forward Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1952 was repealed.
