# Reading Comprehension — Method

## The reality

RC is the largest and most reliably scoring block in the English section, and the one most candidates handle badly by reading too slowly and re-reading too often.

## The method

1. **Read the questions first — only the question stems, not the options.** 20 seconds. You now know what to look for.
2. **Read the passage once, at a normal pace, without stopping to re-read.** Mark (mentally or with a light pencil in your rough sheet) where each idea sits: paragraph 1 = problem, paragraph 2 = counter-argument, paragraph 3 = conclusion.
3. **Answer the fact-based questions first** by returning to the relevant paragraph. These are free.
4. **Then the inference and tone questions**, which need the whole passage.
5. **Never bring outside knowledge.** The correct answer is always supported by the text, even if you believe the text is wrong.

## Question types and how to attack each

| Type | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Fact / detail** | Locate and match. The answer is stated, not implied |
| **Main idea / title** | Must cover the **whole** passage, not one paragraph. Eliminate options that are too narrow or too broad |
| **Inference** | Must follow **necessarily** from the text. Reject anything that needs an extra assumption |
| **Tone / attitude** | Look at adjectives and adverbs. Common answers: critical, appreciative, analytical, objective, sceptical, sarcastic, cautionary |
| **Vocabulary in context** | Substitute each option into the sentence; the word's dictionary meaning is secondary to its use here |
| **Author's purpose** | Why was this written — to argue, describe, criticise, or explain? |
| **Strengthen / weaken** | Find the option that affects the **link** between evidence and conclusion |

## Elimination patterns

Wrong options are usually wrong in predictable ways:
- **Too extreme** — contains "always", "never", "all", "only", "must". Passages rarely support absolutes.
- **Half right** — the first half matches, the second half adds something not in the text. Read the whole option.
- **Out of scope** — true in the world, absent from the passage.
- **Reversed** — states the opposite relationship.
- **Too narrow** — correct about one paragraph, offered as the main idea.

## Speed

Aim for **7–9 minutes per passage with 5 questions** in Phase I. If a passage is dense and unfamiliar, and there is a second passage available, **do the easier one first**. Attempt rate matters more than perfection.

## Practice discipline

One passage a day from an editorial page (The Hindu, Indian Express, Mint) — not from a question bank. Summarise it in **two sentences** after reading. That one habit builds comprehension speed, essay material for Phase II, and current affairs, all at once.

---

## Exam pointers

1. **Question stems first**, options later.
2. **Inference must follow necessarily** — no outside knowledge, no extra assumptions.
3. **Extreme words usually mark a wrong option.**
4. **Half-right options are the main trap** — read every option fully.
5. The main idea must cover the **entire** passage.
