## Multiplication shortcuts

- **×5** → ×10 ÷ 2 · **×25** → ×100 ÷ 4 · **×50** → ×100 ÷ 2 · **×125** → ×1000 ÷ 8
- **×11** → write the digits apart and insert the sums: 43 × 11 → 4 (4+3) 3 = **473**
- **×9, ×99** → ×10 − x, ×100 − x
- **Squares ending in 5:** 65² → 6×7 = 42, append 25 → **4225**
- **(a+b)(a−b) = a² − b²:** 47 × 53 = 50² − 3² = 2500 − 9 = **2491**
- **Numbers near 100:** 96 × 97 → (96−3) | (4×3) = 93|12 = **9312**

             equal profit% and loss% -> net loss x²/100 %
SI/CI        SI = PRT/100 ;  CI = P[(1+R/100)^T − 1]

## Exam-hall discipline

1. **Two passes.** First pass: only questions you can finish in under 45 seconds. Second pass: the rest. Never let one question consume three minutes.
2. **Attempt rate beats accuracy obsession**, up to a point — but with negative marking, never guess blindly on a question you have not read properly.
3. **Write the intermediate step.** Mental arithmetic beyond two steps is where silly errors live.
4. **Track time by block**, not by question: if 20 minutes of your Quant allocation are gone and you have done fewer than 8 questions, move to DI or switch sections.

## Exam pointers

1. Squares to 30 and the fraction table are **non-negotiable recall**.
2. **Two-pass strategy** is the single biggest score improver in Paper 1.
3. **Successive change and CI−SI** formulas appear nearly every year.
4. Use **a² − b²** for products symmetric about a round number.
5. Rewrite this formula sheet **from memory** once a week — that is the actual revision method.
