# Number Series, Quadratic Comparison and Simplification

## Number series

Find the pattern, then the missing or wrong term. Check patterns **in this order**:

1. **Difference** — constant, or an arithmetic pattern in the differences (+2, +4, +6…).
2. **Second-level difference** — differences of differences form a pattern.
3. **Ratio / multiplication** — ×2, ×3, ×1.5, or multiplier changing (×2, ×3, ×4…).
4. **Squares and cubes** — near n², n²±1, n³, n³±n.
5. **Prime numbers** — 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 embedded in the pattern.
6. **Alternating series** — two interleaved patterns; check odd and even positions separately.
7. **Combination** — ×2 then +3, alternately.
8. **Factorials or cumulative sums** — each term adds the previous term.

**Worked examples:**
- 7, 10, 16, 28, 52, ? → differences 3, 6, 12, 24, **48** → answer **100**.
- 4, 9, 25, 49, 121, ? → squares of primes 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, **13** → **169**.
- 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? → n(n+1) → **42**.
- 5, 11, 23, 47, ? → ×2 + 1 → **95**.

**Wrong-term series:** find the pattern from three consecutive terms that fit, then test the rest.

## Quadratic equation comparison

You are given two equations and must compare x and y.

**Method:** factorise both, obtain both roots, and compare **every root of x with every root of y**.

Conclusion options: x > y, x < y, x ≥ y, x ≤ y, or **relationship cannot be established**.

**The key trap:** if the ranges of x and y **overlap** even partially, the answer is "cannot be established". Candidates lose these marks by comparing only the largest roots.

**Quick factorisation:** for x² + bx + c, find two numbers whose product is c and sum is b. Signs: if c is positive, both roots share the sign of b (with opposite sign to b); if c is negative, roots have opposite signs.

## Simplification and approximation

**BODMAS** order: Brackets → Order (powers/roots) → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction.

**Approximation questions** are solved by rounding, not by exact computation:
- √2 = 1.414, √3 = 1.732, √5 = 2.236, √7 = 2.646, π = 3.14
- Squares to 30 and cubes to 15 should be instant recall.
- Convert percentages to fractions before multiplying.

**Speed technique — "approximate then verify the last digit"** works well when options are close: estimate the magnitude, then compute only the unit digit to discriminate.

## Exam pointers

1. Check **differences first, then ratios, then squares/cubes** — in that order.
2. Alternating series: **check odd and even positions separately.**
3. In quadratic comparison, **overlapping ranges mean "cannot be established"**.
4. Compare **all four root pairs**, not just the largest.
5. Squares to 30, cubes to 15, and the fraction-percentage table must be **recall, not calculation**.
