# Indian Financial System — Structure & Regulators

> Why this matters: almost every General Awareness question, and the opening minute of most interviews, assumes you can place SEBI correctly inside the wider system.

## 1. What a financial system does

A financial system moves surplus funds from **savers** to **users of capital**, and in doing so performs four jobs: mobilising savings, allocating capital, managing risk, and providing a payment mechanism. It has four components — **institutions, markets, instruments, services**.

## 2. The four pillars of Indian regulation

| Regulator | Established | Regulates |
|---|---|---|
| RBI | 1 April 1935 (nationalised 1949) | Monetary policy, banks, NBFCs, payment systems, forex, G-sec market |
| SEBI | 12 April 1988; statutory 30 Jan 1992 | Securities markets, intermediaries, listed companies' disclosures |
| IRDAI | 1999 (IRDA Act 1999) | Insurance companies and intermediaries |
| PFRDA | 2003; statutory 2013 | NPS, pension funds, Atal Pension Yojana |

Two more you must know: **IBBI** (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India, 2016, under the IBC) and **IFSCA** (International Financial Services Centres Authority, 2020, the unified regulator for GIFT City).

**FSDC** — Financial Stability and Development Council (2010, non-statutory) — is chaired by the **Union Finance Minister**, with all regulator heads as members. It is the coordination forum for systemic risk; its sub-committee is chaired by the **RBI Governor**.

## 3. Money market vs capital market

| | Money market | Capital market |
|---|---|---|
| Tenure | Up to 1 year | Over 1 year |
| Regulator | RBI | SEBI (RBI for G-sec) |
| Instruments | T-bills, CP, CD, call money, repo, TREPS | Equity, debentures, bonds, derivatives |
| Purpose | Liquidity management | Long-term capital formation |

**Money market instruments:** Treasury Bills (91/182/364 day, issued at a discount, no default risk), Commercial Paper (unsecured promissory note issued by corporates, 7 days to 1 year), Certificate of Deposit (issued by banks/FIs), Call money (1 day, inter-bank), Notice money (2–14 days), Term money (15 days–1 year), Repo and Reverse Repo, TREPS (Triparty Repo, which replaced CBLO in 2018).

## 4. Structure of the capital market

```
Capital Market
├── Primary market (new issues)  -> IPO, FPO, rights, preferential, QIP, private placement
└── Secondary market (trading)   -> stock exchanges, OTC
     ├── Equity segment
     ├── Debt segment
     ├── Derivatives (equity F&O, currency, commodity)
     └── SLB (securities lending and borrowing)
```

## 5. Market participants and intermediaries

- **Issuers** — companies, governments, REITs/InvITs raising money.
- **Investors** — retail, HNI, DII (mutual funds, insurers, pension funds, banks), FPI.
- **SEBI-registered intermediaries** — stock brokers, merchant bankers, registrars to an issue and share transfer agents (RTA), depository participants, underwriters, bankers to an issue, debenture trustees, credit rating agencies, custodians, investment advisers, research analysts, portfolio managers, AIF managers, mutual funds.
- **Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs)** — **stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories**. Systemically important, and held to the strictest governance, technology and cyber standards. *For the IT stream, MIIs are your home turf.*

## 6. Banking system at a glance

Scheduled banks (listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act) split into commercial and cooperative banks. Commercial banks: public sector (12 after the 2020 mergers), private sector, foreign, regional rural banks, small finance banks, and payments banks (which cannot lend; deposit cap raised to ₹2 lakh in 2021).

**NBFCs** are regulated by the RBI, cannot accept demand deposits, offer no cheque facility and carry no deposit insurance. Since October 2022 they sit in a **scale-based regulation** pyramid: Base, Middle, Upper and Top Layer.

## 7. Financial inclusion and development institutions

PMJDY (2014), PMJJBY / PMSBY / APY (2015), MUDRA, Stand-Up India. Development finance: **NABARD** (1982, rural), **SIDBI** (1990, MSME), **EXIM Bank** (1982), **NHB** (1988), **NaBFID** (2021, the infrastructure DFI).

## Exam pointers

1. SEBI is **statutory** since 1992 but was **set up** in 1988. Both dates get asked.
2. FSDC is **non-statutory**, chaired by the **Finance Minister**; its sub-committee by the **RBI Governor**.
3. The G-sec market is regulated by the **RBI**, not SEBI — even though G-secs trade on exchanges (retail access through **RBI Retail Direct**).
4. "MII" is a category, not an institution: exchanges + clearing corporations + depositories.
5. Do not confuse **IRDAI** (insurance) with **IBBI** (insolvency) — the options deliberately swap them.
