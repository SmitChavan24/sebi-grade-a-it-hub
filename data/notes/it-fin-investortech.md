# Investor-Facing Technology and Market Access

## Channels through which investors reach the market

- **Internet-Based Trading (IBT)** and mobile apps, with **two-factor authentication mandated** for client logins.
- **Securities Trading using Wireless Technology (STWT)**.
- **Call and trade**, with recorded lines.
- **Execution-only platforms** for mutual funds; **RIA/distributor** platforms.
- **Online Bond Platform Providers (OBPPs)** — SEBI brought retail bond platforms into a registration framework, addressing unregulated platforms selling debt to retail investors.

## Technology-enabled investor protection

| Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Two-factor authentication** | Mandatory for trading account access |
| **DDPI replacing PoA** | Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction — narrowly scoped authority instead of a broad power of attorney, after misuse of client securities |
| **Direct payout of securities** | Securities credited straight to the client's demat account |
| **Upstreaming of client funds** | Client money moves to the clearing corporation |
| **ASBA-like block for secondary market** | Funds stay in the client's own bank account |
| **SMS/email alerts** from exchanges and depositories on every trade and demat debit | Independent confirmation outside the broker's channel |
| **Consolidated Account Statement (CAS)** | Single view across depositories and mutual funds |
| **Nomination** mandatory or explicit opt-out | Reduces unclaimed assets |
| **Investor charters and disclosure of complaint data** | Transparency on service quality |

> Notice the pattern: several of these controls exist so that an investor receives **independent confirmation** that does not pass through the intermediary being monitored. That design principle — out-of-band verification — is worth naming.

## Account Aggregator and consented data sharing

The **Account Aggregator (AA)** framework lets a user share financial data across institutions with **explicit, revocable, purpose-limited consent**, through an RBI-regulated intermediary that carries data but never stores it. Securities market participants have been joining as **Financial Information Providers/Users**. It is the clearest Indian example of **consent-based data portability**, and it aligns with the DPDP Act's consent architecture.

## Digital fraud targeting investors

The realistic threat list: fake trading apps and cloned websites, **social media tip groups** and unregistered advisers promising assured returns, **deepfake videos** of known market figures, **dabba trading** platforms, fraudulent "IPO allotment" and "refund" messages, remote-access-tool scams, and mule accounts used to launder proceeds.

**Countermeasures:** registration verification tools, SEBI investor alerts and caution lists, mandatory risk disclosures, takedown mechanisms, platform accountability, and investor education. A structural point worth making: **detection at the platform layer scales better than case-by-case enforcement** — which is why regulators press intermediaries and social platforms rather than chasing individual fraudsters alone.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Regional language interfaces, simplified onboarding, low-ticket products (SIPs from ₹100, fractional access debates), and the risk that easy access without understanding pushes retail investors into **derivatives speculation** — which is exactly the harm SEBI's recent disclosures about F&O losses were aimed at.

## Exam pointers

1. **2FA is mandatory** for internet-based trading logins.
2. **DDPI replaced the broad PoA** model after misuse of client securities.
3. Exchange/depository **SMS and email alerts are deliberately out-of-band**.
4. **OBPPs** brought retail bond platforms under registration.
5. **Account Aggregator = consented, revocable, purpose-limited data sharing** — a strong India Stack example.
